Product case study / LendingShipped

Launching a credit line the business didn't want

I took over a contested credit line, designed the controlled experiment and wrote the readout that drove the decision to scale. It hit 3.4x its adoption target at neutral unit economics.

Role
Senior Product Manager, Mexico
Company
Global consumer lending company
Area
Consumer lending
Period
2023 to 2024
  • 3.4xthe adoption target
  • Neutralunit economics, against a forecast loss
  • ~7 in 10adopters came back for more

Context and constraints

In my first month at the company I took over a new credit line that let repeat borrowers finance phone top-ups. The Business team, which also oversaw Legal, had a standing objection to it.

The objection was cannibalization: would a new line eat into revenue from the main loan? The business case accepted a projected loss as R&D, so the product had to prove it did no harm before it could earn a bigger rollout.

My role

MVP scope, experiment design, success criteria, keeping the project resourced through a year of debate, and the readout that drove the decision.

Decision record

  • An additional line, not a discount on the active loan, so the effect on the core product could be measured cleanly.
  • A controlled A/B test with a holdout, restricted to repeat borrowers above a minimum credit limit.
  • Success criteria fixed before launch: adoption, satisfaction and default rate against control, plus an explicit hypothesis that answered Business: repayment would not change.
  • Quantitative plus qualitative: a Sean Ellis survey, CSAT and pricing discovery alongside the experiment.
  • Contribution margin, not isolated loan revenue, because margin was the metric Business actually cared about.

Evidence

Criterion Fixed before launch Result Verdict
Adoption A target set in advance 3.4x the target Met
Default rate No change vs. control No statistically significant difference Met
Satisfaction Sean Ellis threshold Within the margin of error of the threshold Indeterminate

Proof of impact

  • Adoption at 3.4x the target.
  • No statistically significant change in default rate between treatment and control.
  • Neutral contribution margin, against a business case that forecast a loss.
  • About 7 in 10 adopters came back for more than one top-up.
  • Roughly three quarters of users said they’d keep using it with a fee, which set up monetization as the next step.
  • The readout led to scaling the product to a population more than six times the pilot, keeping an explicit holdout. It kept growing after I moved to the platform team.

How to read these results

3.4x is relative to an adoption target set before launch, not absolute growth. Default rate showed no statistically significant change against the control group.

What I’d do differently

Make retention a primary metric from day one, size the sample for every threshold, and write stop criteria next to success criteria. Clear stop criteria turn every review into a decision instead of a negotiation.
