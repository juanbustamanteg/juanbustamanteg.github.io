Work/Tribal Pay

Product case study / PaymentsShipped

Reaching the spend a corporate card can't

As Lead PM I took Tribal Pay from a pilot with fewer than 10 clients to a consolidated revenue line, with US$4M in monthly volume, 350 active businesses and about a fifth of everything Tribal processed.

The challenge
A large share of B2B spend sat outside a card-first platform, and Tribal Pay was a prototype competing with the flagship card for engineers.
My role
Lead PM: strategy, roadmap and launch end to end, from prototype to production to a revenue line.
The call
Local payments in Mexico first; international payments cut from scope.
The outcome
From fewer than 10 pilot clients to 350 active businesses; +20% revenue and +45% retention within 18 months of launch.
  • +20%revenue growth within 18 months of launch
  • +45%customer retention within 18 months of launch
  • ~20%of company processed volume

Context and constraints

Tribal Credit was a fast-scaling, SoftBank-backed fintech serving startups and SMBs in emerging markets. Its flagship was Tribal Card, a multi-currency corporate Visa.

A card only works where it’s accepted. In emerging markets a large share of what a business actually spends goes to suppliers and services that don’t take cards: bank transfers, invoices, bills. That spend sat outside the platform. When I arrived, Tribal Pay was a prototype tested with a handful of clients, competing with the flagship for the same engineering resources.

My role

I owned strategy, roadmap and launch end to end, from prototype to production to a revenue line.

Decision record

Decision record

Local first, international later.

Chosen

Local payments in Mexico

Not chosen

International payments at launch

TradeoffI cut international payments from scope and focused the product on local payments in Mexico. That focus is what took usage off.

  • Rails. I worked with STP and Arcus to integrate the payment rails that local transfers ran on.
  • Earning capacity next to the flagship. Pay competed with the card for the same engineers. I asked for capacity in stages tied to volume milestones, each one unlocking the next, and showed that customers who used Pay stayed longer and spent more on the card. That turned Pay from a competitor into a complement and earned it a dedicated team.
  • Validating with the right first customers. I picked card customers who were paying local suppliers by bank transfer outside the platform, interviewed their finance and operations teams about who they paid and how, onboarded them hands-on, and checked whether they kept paying in months two and three before opening the product up.

Proof of impact

  • 20% revenue growth and a 45% increase in customer retention within 18 months of launch.
  • Churn down by up to 65% over 12 months, after research-driven product improvements.
  • From a pilot with fewer than 10 clients to 350 active businesses.
  • US$4M in monthly transaction volume by mid-2023, US$45M processed from launch to October 2023.
  • Roughly 20% of Tribal’s total processed volume.

How to read these results

Volume figures cover launch through October 2023.

How the team decided

There was no shared way of deciding what to build. I established the product team’s prioritization and discovery practices, and onboarding was how each new PM inherited them. Incoming junior PMs didn’t report to me, so I had no performance lever: I reviewed their PRDs, stories and research before they shipped and coached them through live prioritization and engineering trade-offs. I also helped define the profile for, and interviewed, incoming product hires.
