When friction reads as safety

Before building a new repayment method for Mexico, I tested the concept with borrowers at different credit maturity and found that the default instinct, removing friction, was wrong.

Role
Senior Product Manager, Mexico
Company
Global consumer lending company
Area
Consumer lending · Payments
Period
2024
  • 3credit-maturity segments tested
  • 4usability and trust findings

Context and constraints

We were adding a way for customers in Mexico to repay from their debit account. In lending, repayment methods move repayment rate and collections cost directly.

Validate the concept and the flow before building it, and decide what to change based on what we learned.

My role

I designed and ran the study: research questions, segmentation and moderation.

Approach

Moderated usability tests on a prototype, segmented by credit maturity rather than demographics: first-time borrowers, growing and mature customers. I evaluated usability, concept comprehension and content. It was qualitative research meant to explain why, not to measure how much.

What we found

  • The name didn’t communicate. Half the participants assumed they’d have to type in account numbers and passwords.
  • Friction read as security. Most participants worried about misuse of their money. Paying too easily felt like a risk, not a benefit.
  • “Processing” with no reference point created uncertainty.
  • Limited processing hours felt arbitrary next to other financial institutions, while 24-hour processing was accepted as normal.

Decision record

We rewrote the payment method content so it explained itself, and added proof of each transaction: downloadable receipts, email confirmations and explicit signals that the company never charges without authorization, which until then lived only in the terms and conditions.

Proof of impact

The payment method shipped to production in Mexico and runs with real customers.

How to read these results

Qualitative research with a small moderated sample: it explains why, not how much.

What I’d do differently

Pair qualitative research with launch metrics, so findings can be checked against behavior once the product is live.
